## Equations and limits

The smooth interpolation is `S(t)=6t⁵−15t⁴+10t³` with clamped `t`. The combined exterior weight is `1−(1−S_height)(1−S_radius)`. Time interpolation operates on linear radiance before exposure and tone mapping.

The Sgr A* torus uses a dimensionless height scale `H/Rs = 0.25 + 0.38ρ`, with a Gaussian vertical density and a radial taper. Extinction is exponential, `T=exp(−τ)`, with an analytic small-optical-depth limit for the emission integral. Its assumed prograde rotation period is `2π GM/c³ [(r/(GM/c²))^(3/2)+a*]`. Animation time uses the same mass scaling as the lens coordinates.

The [requested SpaceEngine article](https://spaceengine.org/news/blog220830/) motivates the distinction between a thick, optically thin, low-efficiency flow and a thin disk. Its optical visualizations should not be treated as an EHT radio image. [EHT's Sgr A* model paper](https://eventhorizontelescope.org/publications/first-sagittarius-event-horizon-telescope-results-iv-variability-morphology-and-0) provides the scientific context for the accretion-flow models. Our implementation is an original, bounded optical illustration: it does not solve GRMHD, polarized synchrotron transfer, spectral lines or a measured optical luminosity. Spin, viewing angle, outflow geometry and RGB colour are assumed. The delay uses sampled path length, not full Kerr coordinate-time transport. The many-body/craft dynamics remain the previously documented approximations.

Gargantua's disk is about 1.86 times its previous outer radius. At a **fixed temperature and radius**, its new emission multiplier is 2.13 times the old value; the higher temperature also changes radiance substantially and nonlinearly. HDR retains the existing highlight shoulder; auto exposure retains the existing scene meter. No exposure or lens-flare algorithm is changed here.
